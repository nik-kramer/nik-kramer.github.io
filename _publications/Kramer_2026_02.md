excerpt: '<p style="text-align: justify; font-size: 0.8em;">Market environmentalism has traditionally been the dominant paradigm in EU climate policy. It views climate change as a market failure and a collective action problem, considering carbon pricing the optimal solution. Over the past two decades, its dominance has been challenged by a competing paradigm that builds on the principle of catalytic action. This ‘catalytic decarbonization’ paradigm views climate change as an investment problem and demands industrial policy over carbon pricing. In this paper, I trace these ideational changes in EU climate policy to identify patterns and mechanisms of change (1990-2023). I find that the paradigm change has unfolded progressively. The lack of international commitment to market environmentalism, the momentum after the financial crisis, and the simultaneous diffusion of green industrial policy in the late 2000s and early 2010s destabilized the dominance of market environmentalism and provoked a reorientation in EU climate policy. This shift was consolidated by the adoption of the European Green Deal and further reinforced by the geopoliticization of climate policy in the context of Russia’s war in Ukraine and the intensified geoeconomic competition with China and the US. The EU institutions thereby served as ideational bricoleurs, recombining and repurposing different ideas to adapt to changing circumstances.</p>'
