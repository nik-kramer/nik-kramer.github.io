excerpt: '<p style="text-align: justify">In recent debates about the energy transition, diagnoses of "failure" and calls for a "restart", "reset", and more "realism" have gained traction. We trace the emergence of this discourse in Germany and show that, despite strong terminological overlap, it comprises competing narratives with distinct policy demands. Building on the Narrative Policy Framework and a systematic analysis of 81 media articles and policy documents, we distinguish three narratives that structure the German "restart discourse". The restoration narrative portrays the energy transition as a fundamentally misguided project of a green elite and seeks its rollback. The reorientation narrative criticizes the unbalanced pursuit of climate change mitigation at the cost of economic prosperity and energy security. The recalibration narrative suggests an adjustment of the policy mix in the light of accumulated experience and changing contextual conditions. In addition, we build on the climate obstruction literature to develop five discursive markers and identify obstructive properties in each narrative. Our analysis shows that by 2025 the restart discourse had spread from opinion pieces in conservative and right-wing media to institutional actors such as the federal government and business actors. This mainstreaming reduced the overall prevalence of obstructive properties. However, it also blurred the boundary between obstructive and non-obstructive contestations. This, we argue, benefits obstructionist actors, allowing them to borrow the legitimacy of more moderate, reform-oriented positions while advancing an obstructive agenda.</p>'
