excerpt: '<p style="text-align: justify">In recent debates about the state and prospects of the energy transition, diagnoses of “failure” and calls for a “restart”, “reset” or “reality check” have gained traction. Should these contestations be categorically rejected as climate obstruction, or can some of them contribute constructively to policy learning? To address this question, we examine the German “restart discourse” as an illustrative case to show that, despite strong terminological overlap, the discourse comprises competing narratives with distinct policy demands. Building on the Narrative Policy Framework and a systematic analysis of 81 media articles and policy documents, we distinguish three narratives that structure the restart discourse. The restoration narrative portrays the energy transition as a fundamentally misguided project of a green elite and seeks its rollback. The reorientation narrative criticizes the unbalanced pursuit of climate change mitigation at the cost of economic prosperity and energy security. Finally, the recalibration narrative suggests an adjustment of the policy mix in the light of accumulated experience and changing contextual conditions. Based on this classification, we inductively derive six discursive markers from our sample. In future research, they may be used as a heuristic toolbox for distinguishing obstructive from constructive contestation.</p>'
