excerpt: "<p style=\"text-align: justify\">Can policy proposals generate public backlash even before their costs materialize? We hypothesize that anticipated losses from a salient policy proposal erode citizens' willingness to bear the costs of future policy and their trust in the institutions involved in the policy process. We test this hypothesis using Germany's 2023 heating reform, which included a proposal to ban new fossil-based heating systems and sparked intense public controversy. Four panel survey waves, covering the periods before, during, and after the debate, allow a difference-in-differences comparison between individuals highly exposed to the projected costs and less affected individuals. We find that higher exposure significantly reduced trust in government and willingness to accept higher fossil fuel prices for climate change mitigation. These effects persisted even after the proposed installation ban was postponed. Our findings suggest that policy proposals which threaten concentrated costs can already generate lasting public backlash when the costs are still uncertain.</p>"
