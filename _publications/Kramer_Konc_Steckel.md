excerpt: '<p style="text-align: justify">Climate policies frequently face popular backlash. We build on policy feedback theory to understand the underlying drivers, hypothesizing that exposure to the costs of new climate policy diminishes support for comparable policies and political institutions deemed responsible. We test these propositions for the case of the 2023 German heating reform, which mandated the phase-out of fossil-fuel heating systems in Germany after a contentious public debate. Drawing on survey panel data collected at different stages of the policy process, we find that anticipated exposure to the costs of the policy had no effect on support for other climate policies. However, it caused a significant decline in willingness to pay for further climate policy and trust in government. These effects are particularly pronounced among individuals in low- and middle-income households, suggesting that affordability concerns are a key driver of public backlash to climate policy.</p>'
