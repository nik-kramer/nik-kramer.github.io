excerpt: '<p style="text-align: justify">The decarbonization of energy-intensive industries demands large amounts of low-emissions hydrogen, which will not be available at low prices everywhere. Countries with a high potential for renewable energy can capitalize on these resources to produce renewable hydrogen, while others need to rely on imports to decarbonize their industries. Since transporting hydrogen will remain expensive, firms in the steel and chemical industries, among others, might move production sites to countries that produce cheap low-emissions hydrogen. Thus, hydrogen-based decarbonization creates geoeconomic competition and could unsettle the current distribution of energy-intensive value chains. However, renewable energy resources are not the only determinant for future industrial geographies. In this chapter, we review these determinants, discussing the role of resource endowments, transport networks, and technological and financial capacities. Industrial policies are of particular importance for shaping these markets. We therefore show how governments craft hydrogen strategies to align policy mixes with national economic and security objectives. Building on a typology of strategies, we provide an empirical analysis of multiple country cases. Thereby, we illustrate that industrial decarbonization imposes competition, but also potentials for cooperation.</p>'
