excerpt: '<p style="text-align: justify">Carbon taxes have been adopted in many countries around the world, despite their alleged political unviability. To make sense of this and to improve our understanding of the political conditions for the adoption of carbon taxes, we conduct a systematic review of the academic literature. Using an exhaustive search methodology in combination with machine-learning-assisted abstract screening, we identify relevant actors, their objectives, and context factors from 64 case studies, corresponding to 46 policies across 35 jurisdictions. We find that the adoption of carbon taxes is motivated by a variety of objectives, many of which are unrelated to the original idea of pricing environmental pollution. Clustering the data reveals common barriers and enabling factors across different policy cases. Based on these findings, we argue that carbon taxes are adopted when governments are exposed to problem pressure and able to craft advocacy coalitions that are sufficiently strong to moderate countermobilization and overcome institutional barriers.</p>'
