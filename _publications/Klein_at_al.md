excerpt: '<p style="text-align: justify">Carbon taxes have been widely suggested as a tool for climate change mitigation. Yet, uptake is slow and often diluted by politics. Here, we conduct a systematic review of the case study literature in order to improve our understanding of a conducive context for the adoption of carbon taxes. Using an exhaustive search methodology in combination with machine-learning-assisted abstract screening, we identify, review and classify text data from 64 studies, corresponding to 46 policies across 35 jurisdictions. We find that the adoption of carbon taxes is influenced by a variety of objectives, many of which are unrelated to the original idea of pricing environmental pollution. Clustering the data reveals different political economy landscapes, which exhibit distinct barriers for the adoption of carbon taxes. We highlight successful solutions to overcoming such barriers and point out research gaps in the literature.</p>'
