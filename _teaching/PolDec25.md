<p style="text-align: justify">States have for centuries depended on the extraction of fossil fuels to provide security, prosperity and liberty. By the late twentieth century, however, the acceleration of climate change exposed the destructive effects of this dependency. Tackling climate change now demands a rapid phase-out of fossil fuels and a full decarbonization of the world economy. This is an enormous political and economic challenge. In this seminar, we engage with the respective literature in comparative and international political economy to understand how the transition to a net-zero economy might be realized. Such a transition needs to be not only fast but also just and fair. To learn about the prospects of a just transition, we will conduct a simulation game in which students will play the role of different stakeholders and collectively negotiate a coal phase-out in a fictional fossil fuel-dependent country. In sum, the seminar will provide students with practical and theoretical knowledge to critically assess the potentials and barriers of different decarbonization pathways.</p>
